## Abstract

This paper describes an academic enterprise resource planning (ERP) system for D'Onofrio, a fictitious company of ice cream, chocolates and panettone with a seasonal business, a very extensive network of street vendors and perishable products that depend on the cold chain. All company data are invented. The case starts from four problems: the street vendors' daily settlement is done on paper or in Excel, lots expire without anyone seeing it in time, quality and cold-chain checks leave no record tied to the lot, and management decides late and without consolidated data.

The solution is a web ERP built on Odoo 19 Community and PostgreSQL 16, running in Docker containers, without relying on Enterprise licenses. It was developed with Scrum and PMBOK practices in two phases separated by a checkpoint (Gate 0): a guaranteed core and four custom modules that were built one at a time, each with its own mini gate. The core covers the business areas of the case with standard Odoo, configured entirely through code in a single module, `donofrio_base`: marketing and sales (a CRM funnel, price lists by channel and season, and a point of sale with receipts), logistics (lots with expiry dates and first-expired-first-out picking, two-step receiving and dispatch, and replenishment to Arequipa), production (orders with lot and cost), management (dashboards and saved views) and the extras of purchasing, invoicing with VAT, human resources and an IT service desk. It also provides a PostgreSQL replica, backups with a tested restore, and a hardened deployment behind Nginx with TLS, two-factor authentication and an audit log. Phase 2 adds quality inspection with a PDF certificate and blocking of rejected lots (`donofrio_calidad`), the daily settlement of street vendors with a sales order and a PDF (`donofrio_heladeros`), the cold chain with temperature ranges and alerts (inside `donofrio_calidad`) and a custom OWL management dashboard with the company's branding (`donofrio_tablero`).

The results can be verified in the repository. We completed 115 of the 121 user-story points in the plan (95 %), the four Phase 2 modules are published in releases `v1.1.0` to `v1.4.0` (the core is in `v1.0.0`), and all 218 automated tests pass in continuous integration. A restore from backup took 31 seconds against a two-hour target; under a load test with 20 virtual users, the 95th-percentile response time was 11.6 ms for lists and 9.1 ms for forms, against a limit of 2 s; a security scan found no high-risk alerts; and the web performance score of the sales dashboard rose from 45 to 80 with Nginx. The back end (59) and the custom dashboard (76 on desktop, with a load time of 2.11 s that meets the target of under 3 s) stayed below the target score of 80: we report this as a known limit, because it is explained by Odoo's JavaScript and not by the team's code. Measurements come from a single machine with demonstration data.

**Keywords:** Odoo 19; ERP; PostgreSQL; Scrum; Docker.
